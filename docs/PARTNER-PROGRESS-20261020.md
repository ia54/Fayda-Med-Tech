# Fayda MedTech: a working foundation with a clear next step

**We have moved beyond a concept. The synthetic pilot has been accepted, and MedTech now demonstrates substantial working workflows across pharmacy, case management, provider billing and financial records.**

The immediate opportunity is to show partners how these pieces support a more organized operation—and use their feedback to guide a focused release process. This is an encouraging milestone for a platform with a genuinely demanding scope: different professional roles, sensitive documents, financial history and pharmacy records must all work together while preserving the right boundaries.

## Pharmacy is the operational heart

The pharmacy foundation begins with the patient and prescription. It retains ordinary stock information, pharmacist review, preparation and handover evidence. These records provide the operational context around which the wider MedTech platform can develop.

The demonstration includes actual screens showing a synthetic ordinary prescription, its retained fill history and stock record. The completed pharmacy history was prepared through the existing tested workflow before recording; no medicine was dispensed. Advanced controlled-substance and compounding development previews are outside this walkthrough.

The pharmacy and billing examples are shown separately. The recording does not claim that every step between them is already automated or fully integrated.

## What partners can see working

- **Case-based teamwork:** an assigned case gives the legal team a shared starting point.
- **Provider-to-billing handoff:** a provider saves an invoice draft and sends it for internal billing review.
- **Traceable receipt records:** billing staff record money received elsewhere and correct an entry while preserving the original history. MedTech does not collect or transfer money in this demonstration.
- **A clearer client view:** clients see relevant invoices and recorded balances without seeing other clients' records.
- **Settlement planning and reporting:** the team records an allocation, retains a later correction and exports a CSV summary.
- **Private documents:** authorized users upload and preview a synthetic case document in the workspace.
- **Billing follow-up:** staff maintain manual EOB records and create a local appeal draft for human review.

These are working software journeys, demonstrated with synthetic records and test sessions. They are not slides standing in for unbuilt features.

## Why this has commercial value

MedTech addresses the friction between people and systems. A provider's work, a case team's decisions, a client's documents and a biller's follow-up are often separated. Bringing them into a coherent process can reduce repeated handoffs, make outstanding work easier to follow and improve the quality of the record available to each team.

Retained corrections are especially valuable: staff can explain what changed and why without losing the earlier record. Role-specific views help each participant focus on the work they are responsible for. Reports and exports make the information usable beyond the individual screen.

These are credible product benefits and a basis for partner discussions. Actual efficiency gains, customer demand and commercial returns still need to be measured in real operations; no revenue or valuation claim is implied.

## How close are we?

**The core is demonstrable now. The next stage is release preparation and operational acceptance—not starting the product from scratch.**

The owner has accepted the synthetic pilot. The saved environment has been restored independently, core browser journeys have been repeated, and release checks and synthetic recovery exercises have passed. That gives the team a solid base for a useful partner demonstration.

A production launch still needs several specific decisions and checks:

1. Review partner feedback and agree the first operational release scope.
2. Resolve the remaining dependency findings, or obtain an explicit decision from the responsible release owner.
3. Confirm approved hosting access, backups, restoration, migration and rollback procedures.
4. Verify real account recovery, mail delivery, support and operating ownership.
5. Configure and accept any live provider connections included in that release.
6. Complete the applicable pharmacy/site review and obtain separate approval before any real operation or dispensing.

These are important finalizing and operating steps, not a reason to overlook the working software already in place. They also prevent an honest progress report from becoming an unsupported promise of launch timing. We are not assigning a product-completion percentage or a release date before those inputs are known.
